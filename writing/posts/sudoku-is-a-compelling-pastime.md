### Why do I play?:
During my 5 year BS-MS program at [IISER Pune](https://www.iiserpune.ac.in/) (extremely high ROI experience), I had to go through courses from Physics, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry and a couple courses each in Humanities and Earth & Climate Sciences during my first two years. Out of them all, the sharpest jump was in Math. I thought Math was easy, a "plug-and-chug" computation of integrals, matrices and the simple joy of finding x. My first semester course, MTH: 101 Single Variable Calculus, gave me a wry shock. The shift was from *how* to calculate to *why* things work. I had to prove why  $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x}{x} = 1$, I could no longer say, *cuz it is*. 1+1=2 was no longer because my teachers said so, but because we had agreed on the Peano axioms and it demanded it.

I saw Math as a sandbox of rules that we used to deductively construct skyscrapers. It grew on me the more I engaged with it and with my peers who had already started worshipping the Epsilon-Delta deities. Of course, there were courses where I couldn't figure out how the proofs worked and with a day left for the exams, I could do nothing but memorize those few proofs (I'm looking at you Functional Analysis). But third year came, and now we got to choose our own courses, like it was some buffet now that we had a taste of the main dishes before the feast was ready. While I majorly chose Physics courses, I had gotten a taste of this rigorous math, and I wanted a little bit constantly, like oorga with curd rice. I compensated with some approachable math courses that satisfied this itch. 

Once, I graduated I was going into a Data Science PhD program, pure distilled crystal math courses felt like a distraction from my research. Yet, the itch remained. I would feel like those synapses of my brain that were put through fire to be able to deal with mathematical rigour where slowly dying and I could do nothing but watch them rot. I tried Brilliant.org (too random or easy) and math subreddits or stackexchanges, a pre-LLM concept (too complex), and finally I found my "fix" in a daily ritual I engaged in when at home - reading *The Hindu*:

One day, pen in hand, I saw the oft ignored sudoku section. It had a four star rating, I just need to fill in 1-9 in each row, column and 3x3 box without overlap, how hard can it be I thought.

I failed. I started again, and without realizing it, I started doing what I'd do when I had to prove one of them theorems: start with the "given", form small lemmas from the axioms, use those to glean more information, and as more information is revealed, build on top of them with what you know to keep going till you reach the insanely satisfying two words "Hence, proved". This was an astounding exercise. But just as I thought I had cracked the system, a five-star showed up, and my lemmas didn't work, I had to find a new set of them. After 50 minutes I had gotten it.
The next day, it went back to 1 star, but this was trivial and I couldn't wait four more days. So I resorted to online archives of these standard sudoku puzzles, and as I was doing them the depth in the possible strategies were astounding. 

But after a point I did get tired of solving the same set of "givens" in increasingly opaque ways, and right then YouTube reeled in a Giant Marlin. It recommended a video of *Cracking the Cryptic* with the thumbnail of a sudoku squiggles but no digits at all! I opened it to discover the rabbit hole goes deeper! These were handcrafted Variant sudoku puzzles alright, but they were abstracted more by having these additional constraints. Each new constraint or a combination of them can behave completely different and would need me to start thinking again like I was at the start of a fresh proof, searching for the right direction. **This was crack**. 


#### How to get started?
[People might diss on sudoku players](https://medium.com/the-haven/other-activities-i-imagine-sudoku-players-do-for-fun-19de2dead7ed) but that maybe because they've never tried intently. Well first we need to get you a *why* should I do a sudoku if you haven't been convinced yet. It could be as trivial as *cuz it is fun*, for which we need to start with some easy ones.

Sudoku has had other benefits too: 
It sharpened my focus to do uninterrupted, no music 30-40 minute sessions,
Reminded me of perseverance to get at a solution (once I know there exists one)
Practicing going back to the drawing board and starting again differently
Maintaining long trains of thought
With LLMs taking over cognitive functions, this is a little bit like doing rowing for the mind.
